Control in biophysics

Fibril mass grows as $\dot{y}=\alpha[u]+\kappa[u]\,y$, and the total cost $\int_{0}^{T}(y+\zeta u)\,\dd t$ weighs aggregate against drug toxicity. Bang-bang dosing gives $u(t)=u_{\text{max}}$ for $T_{1}<t<T_{2}$ and $u(t)=0$ otherwise. A drug targeting primary nucleation has $\alpha[u]=\alpha_{0}/(1+K^{\text{eq}}_{1}u)^{n_{1}}$ and $\kappa=\kappa_{0}$, and the optimum is $T_{1}=0$, $T_{2}=T-\kappa_{0}^{-1}\ln[\zeta u_{\text{max}}\kappa_{0}/(\alpha_{0}-\alpha)]$. A drug targeting secondary nucleation has $\kappa[u]=\kappa_{0}/(1+K^{\text{eq}}_{2}u)^{n_{2}/2}$ and $\alpha=\alpha_{0}$, and the optimum is a window of length $\Delta T=\frac{\kappa_{0}}{\kappa_{0}-\kappa}\big[T-\kappa_{0}^{-1}\ln\frac{\zeta u_{\text{max}}\kappa_{0}^{2}}{\alpha_{0}(\kappa_{0}-\kappa)}\big]$ centred in $[0,T]$.
