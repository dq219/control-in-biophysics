Theory

Notation follows Sec. 2 (Feedback control) of the review.

The PID controller

The controller keeps a state $y(t)$ at the set value $y^{*}$ using only the error signal

$$\epsilon(t)\equiv y^{*}-y(t),$$

its integral and its time derivative:

$$u(t)=c_{\text{P}}\epsilon(t)+c_{\text{I}}\int_{0}^{t}\epsilon(t')\dd t'+c_{\text{D}}\dot{\epsilon}(t).$$

No model of how $y$ evolves is needed, which is why PID control is model-agnostic.

Linearised error dynamics

For $\dot{y}(t)=f(y,u)+\eta(t)$ with $f(y^{*},0)=0$, expanding to first order about the set point gives

$$\dot{\epsilon}(t)\approx f_{y}\epsilon(t)-f_{u}u(t)-\eta(t),$$

with $f_{y}\equiv\partial f/\partial y$ and $f_{u}\equiv\partial f/\partial u$ evaluated at $y=y^{*}$, $u=0$. This is the system simulated here.

Proportional term

With $c_{\text{I}}=c_{\text{D}}=0$,

$$\dot{\epsilon}(t)=(f_{y}-f_{u}c_{\text{P}})\epsilon(t)-\eta(t).$$

The error decays with characteristic time $-1/(f_{y}-f_{u}c_{\text{P}})$. For white noise $\langle\eta(t)\eta(t')\rangle=\Gamma\delta(t-t')$ the error settles to a Gaussian of variance $\Gamma/[2(f_{u}c_{\text{P}}-f_{y})]$. A slow disturbance leaves a residual offset

$$\epsilon(t)\approx\frac{\eta(t)}{f_{y}-f_{u}c_{\text{P}}},$$

so the P term alone cannot cancel a sustained perturbation. Try the step disturbance with the I switch off.

Integral term

Differentiating once more, for $c_{\text{I}}\neq0$,

$$\ddot{\epsilon}(t)=(f_{y}-f_{u}c_{\text{P}})\dot{\epsilon}(t)-f_{u}c_{\text{I}}\epsilon(t)-\dot{\eta}(t).$$

When $\dot\eta\approx0$ this is a damped harmonic oscillator with modes

$$\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{f_{y}-f_{u}c_{\text{P}}}{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{f_{y}-f_{u}c_{\text{P}}}{2}\right)^{2}-f_{u}c_{\text{I}}}.$$

At $c_{\text{I}}=0$ one mode is $\lambda_{+}=0$, the offset that survives P control. Increasing $f_{u}c_{\text{I}}$ makes the loop overdamped, then critically damped at $f_{u}c_{\text{I}}=[(f_{y}-f_{u}c_{\text{P}})/2]^{2}$, then underdamped.

Derivative term

$$(1+f_{u}c_{\text{D}})\ddot{\epsilon}(t)=(f_{y}-f_{u}c_{\text{P}})\dot{\epsilon}(t)-f_{u}c_{\text{I}}\epsilon(t)-\dot{\eta}(t).$$

$c_{\text{D}}$ adds no new mode. It rescales the effective inertia of the oscillator, slowing and damping the response.

Identified glucose–insulin dynamics

Glucose is sampled every $\Delta t=5$ min, $y_{k}\equiv y(k\Delta t)$, measured in mg/dL from the target. The identified discrete transfer function from insulin to glucose is

$$\bar{G}(z)=\frac{-0.25z^{-3}}{(1-0.98z^{-1})(1-0.75z^{-1})^{2}},$$

which corresponds to the update

$$y_{k}=\alpha_{1}y_{k-1}+\alpha_{2}y_{k-2}+\alpha_{3}y_{k-3}+\beta_{3}u_{k-3}+\eta_{k},$$

with $\alpha_{1}=2.48$, $\alpha_{2}=-2.0325$, $\alpha_{3}=0.55125$ and $\beta_{3}=-0.25$ (mg/dL)/(TDI/hr). Here $u_k$ is the insulin rate in TDI/hr (plotted per 5 min) and $\eta_{k}$ is glucose intake not captured by $\bar{G}$.

SOPTD model and controller tuning

Pole matching with $z=e^{\Delta t s}$, steady-state gain matching, and Skogestad's half rule give a second-order-plus-time-delay model (times in minutes):

$$G(s)=\frac{Ke^{-\theta s}}{(1+\tau_{1}s)(1+\tau_{2}s)}\approx\frac{-200e^{-11s}}{(1+247s)(1+26s)}.$$

Requiring the ideal closed-loop response $Y(s)/Y^{*}(s)=e^{-\theta s}/(1+\tau_{\text{c}}s)$ fixes the gains:

$$c_{\text{P}}=\frac{\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}}{K(\tau_{\text{c}}+\theta)},\qquad c_{\text{I}}=\frac{1}{K(\tau_{\text{c}}+\theta)},\qquad c_{\text{D}}=\frac{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}}{K(\tau_{\text{c}}+\theta)}.$$

$\tau_{\text{c}}$ trades speed for robustness. Typical values are 40 to 150 min.

Discrete (velocity-form) controller

$$u_{k}=u_{k-1}+\Delta P_{k}+\Delta I_{k}+\Delta D_{k},$$ $$\Delta P_{k}\equiv c_{\text{P}}(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{k-1}),\quad \Delta I_{k}\equiv c_{\text{I}}\epsilon_{k}\Delta t,\quad \Delta D_{k}\equiv c_{\text{D}}(\epsilon_{k}-2\epsilon_{k-1}+\epsilon_{k-2})/\Delta t,$$

with $\epsilon_{k}\equiv y_{k}^{*}-y_{k}$ and $y_{k}^{*}=0$. Because insulin cannot be removed, $u_k$ is clipped at zero.

Closed-loop poles

Combining the plant and the controller (without clipping or filtering), the closed-loop poles are the roots of

$$(1-z^{-1})\bigl(1-\alpha_{1}z^{-1}-\alpha_{2}z^{-2}-\alpha_{3}z^{-3}\bigr)+\beta_{3}z^{-3}Q(z^{-1})=0,$$ $$Q(z^{-1})=c_{\text{P}}(1-z^{-1})+c_{\text{I}}\Delta t+c_{\text{D}}(1-z^{-1})^{2}/\Delta t.$$

This characteristic equation is derived for this page and does not appear in the review. Without the I term, $z=1$ is always a root: insulin holds its last value and never relaxes.

Practical considerations

Integral windup. A meal always raises glucose, so the integral error builds up. The resulting extra insulin can push glucose below target, so compare the undershoot of PID with PD. Filtered derivative. With sensor noise $\sigma>0$ the $\Delta D_k$ term amplifies fluctuations. The filter replaces it by $\beta\Delta D_{k-1}+(1-\beta)\Delta D_k$. Parameter drift. The sensitivity $s$ scales the plant's $\beta_3$ while the controller keeps its nominal gains. This mimics the drift that motivates gain scheduling and adaptive PID.
